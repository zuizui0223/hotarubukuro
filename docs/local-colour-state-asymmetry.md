# Post hoc local colour-state asymmetry diagnostic

## Question

The locked local-isolate analysis detects **pigmented focal cells among environmentally similar white neighbours**. It does not test the reverse event. This diagnostic asks whether local colour-state discordance is directionally asymmetric:

1. pigmented focal cells among white neighbours (`pigmented_in_white`); and
2. white focal cells among pigmented neighbours (`white_in_pigmented`).

The purpose is to determine whether the apparent directional pattern exceeds the asymmetry already generated by the fitted environment-plus-SPDE natural model, observed trial counts, spatial sampling frame, and local graph.

## Reused design

The diagnostic reuses the locked primary local-isolate graph without choosing a new spatial scale:

- 10-km neighbourhood radius;
- environmental RMS-distance caliper of 1 across the four broad/within 50-km natural-environment PCs;
- at least three eligible neighbours; and
- the same fold-boundary setting as the locked primary event.

The same event extractor is applied to the observed map and to every cross-fitted natural predictive map.

## Primary estimand

Raw event counts are not directly comparable because the numbers of graph-supported white and pigmented focal cells differ. Each event direction is therefore normalized by its corresponding focal-state opportunity:

- `pigmented_in_white_rate = (P-in-W count + 0.5) / (eligible P focals + 1)`;
- `white_in_pigmented_rate = (W-in-P count + 0.5) / (eligible W focals + 1)`.

The primary asymmetry statistic is:

```text
log_rate_ratio = log(pigmented_in_white_rate / white_in_pigmented_rate)
```

Positive values indicate relatively more pigmented-in-white events. The observed statistic is compared with its distribution across 1,000 natural predictive maps.

## State definitions

The primary rule mirrors the locked event:

- pigmented: at least one pigmented observation in the cell;
- white: zero pigmented observations.

Two classification sensitivities are reported:

- pure cells only (pigmented share 1 versus 0); and
- 10/90 thresholds (share <=0.10 versus >=0.90).

These sensitivities reveal whether mixed cells create the apparent directionality.

## Claim ceiling

This analysis is a **post hoc symmetry diagnostic**, not a pre-specified confirmatory test. Even a strong positive asymmetry would show only that one direction of local state discordance is more common than expected under the fitted natural baseline. It would not identify:

- mutation or developmental transition rates;
- gene flow or population history;
- horticultural introduction or planting;
- pollinator-mediated selection; or
- an evolutionary transition from white to pigmented flowers.

Any mechanism would require field provenance, repeated population sampling, genetics, calibrated colour measurements, and direct ecological observations.
