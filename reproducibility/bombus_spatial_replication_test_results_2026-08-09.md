This is a post-hoc spatial-overlap diagnostic. It does not establish causal pollinator selection. Its purpose is to ask whether the exact coincidence of sharp flower-colour boundaries and predicted Bombus-community boundaries is stronger than expected from nearby edges with comparable geographic/elevational context.

## Primary spatial-match result

The primary fixed design used pure white-versus-pigmented transitions in the 5-km local graph, non-overlapping transition edges, a 50-km background window, 20 nearest matched controls per transition (minimum 10 available), and five-species occurrence-referenced Hellinger community turnover. Controls were selected without Bombus values and matched on edge length, midpoint elevation and absolute elevation difference.

- retained transition edges: 55;
- observed mean community turnover: 0.12720;
- matched-background mean turnover: 0.11049;
- mean matched excess: +0.03303;
- median matched excess: +0.01125;
- transitions with positive matched excess: 60.0%;
- one-sided matched-background P = 0.06283;
- BH q across the 5/10/25-km scale family = 0.06283.

Thus the strict occurrence-referenced 5-km primary test is positive but just outside the conventional 0.05 threshold. It should not be described as a stand-alone significant confirmation.

## Scale replication under the same fixed match specification

Occurrence-referenced Hellinger community turnover:

- 5 km: n = 55, mean excess +0.03303, median excess +0.01125, 60.0% positive, P = 0.06283, q = 0.06283;
- 10 km: n = 102, mean excess +0.03268, median excess +0.00871, 58.8% positive, P = 0.01423, q = 0.02135;
- 25 km: n = 165, mean excess +0.04107, median excess +0.01946, 60.6% positive, P = 0.00010, q = 0.00030.

The excess is therefore directionally consistent at all three scales and is clearly distinguishable from the local spatial/elevational matched background at 10 and 25 km.

The pre-existing within-species-rank Hellinger metric gives the same direction and is significant at all three scales under the same matching specification:

- 5 km: mean excess +0.01645, P = 0.03658;
- 10 km: mean excess +0.01907, P = 0.00802;
- 25 km: mean excess +0.03321, P = 0.00001.

Across all predeclared combinations of 25/50/100-km background windows and 10/20/50 matched controls, the mean matched excess remained positive for both community metrics at every 5/10/25-km scale. Statistical strength varied with the matching set size at 5 and 10 km, while the 25-km result was positive and nominally significant in every occurrence-referenced matching specification. This pattern argues against a result generated by one particular matching bandwidth, while preserving the primary 5-km P = 0.06283 as the primary result.

## Geographic repetition rather than one national boundary

For the primary occurrence-referenced match specification, case-specific matched excess was summarized in fixed 100-km spatial blocks.

At 5 km:

- unshifted grid: 6 of 7 blocks with >=3 transitions had positive mean excess;
- 50-km shifted grid: 7 of 9 blocks had positive mean excess;
- every leave-one-block-out national mean remained positive in both grids;
- minimum leave-one-block-out mean excess = +0.02381.

At 10 km:

- unshifted: 8/11 blocks positive;
- shifted: 12/14 blocks positive;
- all leave-one-block-out means positive (minimum +0.02861 and +0.03064).

At 25 km:

- unshifted: 12/18 blocks positive;
- shifted: 15/19 blocks positive;
- all leave-one-block-out means positive (minimum +0.03736 and +0.03672).

The community-boundary excess is therefore not carried by a single geographic block. Combined with the local spatial/elevational matching, the data support the narrower statement that broad geographic location/elevation alone is insufficient to reproduce the full observed coincidence of flower-colour and predicted Bombus-community boundaries. They do **not** show that Bombus caused the flower-colour boundary, because the community surfaces remain SDM-derived and may share finer unmeasured environmental structure with flower colour.

## Montane/alpine Bombus guardrail

The apparent high-elevation association of *B. beaticola*, *B. consobrinus* and *B. honshuensis* was tested only as a sensitivity diagnostic. Once pure flower-colour transitions were restricted to near-equal elevations, there was no positive montane/alpine residual signal.

All pure transitions with absolute elevation difference <=50 m:

- 5 km: n = 23, mean pigmented-minus-white montane support = -0.00333, P = 0.84321;
- 10 km: n = 29, mean = -0.00196, P = 0.75510;
- 25 km: n = 36, mean = -0.00589, P = 0.83179;
- BH q across scales = 0.84321.

With the <=100-m restriction the means were approximately zero or negative and all P >= 0.47. The conditional subset in which the documented effective guild did not increase toward the pigmented side was also non-significant at every scale/elevation restriction.

**Interpretation:** the earlier montane/alpine association is adequately explained as shared high-elevation/spatial co-distribution in the present data. There is no evidence here for an additional montane Bombus effect or for a substitution mechanism. These species should remain Supplement-only and should not receive a mechanistic Discussion unless independent field evidence becomes available.

## Manuscript-level interpretation

The current integrated interpretation separates the directional mechanism-oriented test from unsigned community biogeography:

- broad environment and spatial structure describe where the major flower-colour geography occurs;
- within that template, the strictest local white-pigmented transitions show weak directional consistency with predicted availability of the two documented focal Bombus pollinators;
- in Supporting Information, sharp flower-colour boundaries also coincide with predicted five-species Bombus-community boundaries more than some nearby spatial/elevational controls, indicating landscape-scale biogeographic correspondence rather than a second colour-selection mechanism;
- the montane/alpine equal-elevation guardrail is negative and prevents high-elevation map overlap from being promoted as an additional mechanism.

The community result is therefore corroborating context for heterogeneity in predicted pollinator assemblages. It does not strengthen the directional Main effect into causal evidence and does not alter the Main claim ceiling.
