# Local-departure placement null: design and interpretation

Status: analysis specification fixed before the placement statistic was run.

## The three questions are different

| Layer | Existing/new statistic | Null comparison | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| v20 frequency | number and fraction of local pigmented departures | the same event detector replayed on 10,000 locked natural posterior-predictive maps | Are there more departures than the natural model produces? |
| v21 local context | each observed candidate minus its own white-neighbour mean | the same focal-minus-neighbour contrast in 10,000 natural maps | Is a candidate more human-exposed than the immediately surrounding white cells? |
| v22 placement | mean human exposure of the candidate set | candidate sets produced by the same event detector in natural maps, primarily restricted to maps with exactly the observed candidate count | Conditional on producing the same number of departures, does the natural model place them in less human-exposed locations than observed? |

The new test does not replace the first two. Candidate frequency, within-locality
contrast, and national placement are distinct estimands and are reported separately.

## Why the placement null is better matched to the human-impact claim

The v21 neighbour contrast is a useful local descriptive control, but its reference
set is only the candidate's own white neighbours. It therefore does not directly
ask where the natural process would place departure events across the full sampled
landscape. A candidate can have a positive local population contrast simply because
settlement exposure varies sharply within a locality, even when such local edges are
common in natural posterior-predictive maps.

The v22 null carries the following structures into every comparison:

1. the locked final-eight-axis natural pigmentation model;
2. its spatially and environmentally structured posterior uncertainty;
3. the same 10-km final-eight-axis neighbourhood graph;
4. the same support and all-neighbours-white event detector;
5. the candidate availability and geographic arrangement of all sampled cells.

Human variables are joined only after each map's candidate set is fixed. They cannot
create, remove, rank, or tune candidates. The primary null uses only simulated maps
with exactly 16 candidates, so the placement statistic is not driven by simulated
maps having unusually few or many events. If fewer than 200 exact-count maps exist,
a preregistered deterministic nearest-count fallback uses up to 1,000 maps; both the
mode and the count difference are reported.

This makes the new test superior for the narrow claim **"observed departures occupy
more human-exposed positions than departures generated by the natural model"**. It
is not intrinsically superior for the different questions of event frequency or
within-neighbourhood contrast.

## Registered outcomes

The primary outcome is the mean rank of 2-km WorldPop exposure across the 16 observed
candidates, compared directionally with the exact-count natural placement null. The
2-km layer is reconstructed from the historically frozen WorldPop declaration and
verified against its recorded SHA-256 before analysis.

Multiplicity is shown at two levels:

- maxT across the 2, 5, 10, 25, and 50-km population family;
- maxT across the full human-context registry: population scales, DID proximity,
  road proximity, built-up fraction, and forest-human edge.

Forest cover and mountainness are natural-alternative controls. Observation effort
and independent-site support are observation-process controls. The mean is primary;
the median and all-nonempty-map null are fixed sensitivity analyses.

## Decision rule

- `directional P < 0.05` and global-human `maxT FWER P < 0.05`: placement support
  robust to the registered human family.
- `directional P < 0.05` but global-human `maxT FWER P >= 0.05`: localized or
  suggestive placement evidence, not familywise-confirmed human context.
- `directional P >= 0.05`: no support for excess human-oriented placement under the
  locked natural null.

The population-scale familywise result is reported in addition to, not instead of,
the global-human correction.

## Inference ceiling

Even a supported placement departure does not identify provenance. It cannot by
itself distinguish horticultural escape, planting, introgression, phenotypic
plasticity, unmeasured natural environment, or observation access. It is an event
placement diagnostic for prioritizing field-history and genetic checks, not proof
that humans caused the flower colour.
