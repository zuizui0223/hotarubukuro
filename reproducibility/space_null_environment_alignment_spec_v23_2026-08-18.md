# Specification: environment-aligned divergence beyond spatial continuity (v23)

**Central hypothesis.** At comparable geographical separation, phenotype divergence between locations increases with environmental difference **beyond** what a model containing only an intercept and a continuous Matérn field predicts.

**Spec version:** `v23.0_environment_alignment_beyond_space`
**Relation to existing work:** extends the merged PR #50 sensitivity. The published result (5 geographic bins, all strata) remains the primary reported analysis. This document adds stratification sensitivities and a confound diagnostic. Nothing is withdrawn.

---

## 1. Why this is the right central hypothesis

The broad stage produces two things that are easy to confuse. Coefficients say *which* environmental axes are associated with flower colour. The spatial field says *how much* coherent geography remains once those axes are used. Neither answers the question that matters for adaptation: **is the environmental association something more than the fact that nearby places resemble each other?**

That question needs a different estimand. Two locations can be similar because they are close, because they share climate, or both. The test therefore holds geographical separation approximately constant and asks whether environmental difference still orders phenotype divergence.

### 1.1 What is borrowed from PST > FST, and what is not

The **logic** is borrowed: establish a background expectation generated by processes other than environmental sorting, then ask whether trait divergence exceeds it.

The **structure** is not. PST > FST compares two commensurate divergence statistics, one from traits and one from neutral markers. Here the background is a fitted spatial predictive distribution, not a divergence statistic, and no genetic data enter. The spatial field is unresolved geography — it may contain population history, dispersal, unmeasured environment and sampling geometry together, and it is not interpretable as drift.

The manuscript should therefore say the analysis is FST/PST-inspired **and state this difference explicitly**, rather than letting the analogy imply a divergence comparison.

### 1.2 The confound this design must survive

Environment is not spatially random: nearby places tend to share climate. If environmental distance were simply a proxy for geographical distance, the test would rediscover spatial continuity and call it environmental alignment.

This was measured directly on the frozen cell table (850,860 pairs, eight standardised abiotic axes):

| Scale | Spearman(geographic distance, environmental distance) |
|---|---:|
| all pairs | **0.093** |
| within geographic quintile 1 (1–134 km) | **0.365** |
| quintile 2 (134–234 km) | 0.005 |
| quintile 3 (234–356 km) | −0.055 |
| quintile 4 (356–546 km) | −0.020 |
| quintile 5 (546–1322 km) | 0.009 |

Within quintile 1, pairs in the upper environmental-distance quartile are on average **33.6 km further apart** than those in the lower quartile; in every other quintile the gap is under 6 km. Splitting quintile 1 into ten finer bins leaves the correlation only in the closest bin (0.325) and near zero elsewhere (−0.004 to 0.083).

**The confound exists, is real, and is confined to the shortest distances.** Japan's north–south extent and steep elevation gradients keep the overall association weak, which is a favourable geography for separating isolation-by-distance from isolation-by-environment.

## 2. Predictions

| | Prediction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | The observed excess for pigmentation state is not produced by the residual geography–environment correlation | if it were, removing the confounded stratum would reduce or remove it |
| **P2** | The excess is stable to finer geographical stratification | finer bins hold separation more tightly constant |
| **P3** | Conditional intensity shows no excess under any stratification | the state/intensity distinction is a property of the traits, not of the binning |

P1 and P2 can each fail. Failure of either is reported as a limitation of the published result, not repaired.

## 4. What this specification varies

Exactly one parameter, already exposed by the analysis script as `--geo-bins` (default 5), plus one stratum-subset rule.

| Run | Bins | Strata used |
|---|---:|---|
| **A (published, primary)** | 5 | all |
| B | 5 | closest stratum dropped |
| C | 10 | all |
| D | 10 | closest stratum dropped |
| E | 20 | all |
| F | 20 | closest stratum dropped |

Run A remains the primary reported result regardless of what B–F show. B–F are sensitivities. No run is promoted on the basis of its P value.

**Rationale for the drop rule:** the diagnostic in §1.2 identifies the closest stratum as the only one where separation is not effectively held constant. Removing it implements the design's stated intent more faithfully; it is not a search over subsets.

The **observed side** of runs A–F was computed locally on 2026-08-18 before this specification was committed. The null side was not: INLA is unavailable in that environment. Full disclosure of what is already known:

|---|---:|---:|
| A (5 bins, all) | +0.106357 | −0.046485 |

| F (20 bins, drop closest) | +0.109671 | −0.057001 |

Run A reproduces the published observed contrast (+0.106802) to within 0.0005, the difference arising from a re-implementation of the pair sampling. The state excess does not weaken under finer stratification or removal of the confounded stratum; it increases. Intensity remains negative throughout.

**These are observed contrasts only. No P value is available for B–F until the null is regenerated.** Because the excess is defined relative to a null that must be refitted under each binning, none of these numbers is a result on its own.

## 6. Reporting rules, fixed in advance

- Run A remains primary in Main Results; B–F appear as a stratification sensitivity table.
- If the state excess loses significance under C or D, this is reported as a limitation of the published result. The published P=0.03393 is not withdrawn but is qualified.
- The published result is **borderline**: the observed statistic exceeds the 95th percentile of the space-only null but not the 97.5th, so it is significant only under the predeclared one-sided 5% rule. State this in the main text, not only in the appendix.
- No stratification is described as significant in isolation; the sensitivity is read as a profile.
- H1 is supported by four paths — coefficient structure, differing residual spatial ranges, this sensitivity, and the independent *Bombus* predictor class. This test is not asked to carry H1 alone.

---

## 7. Claim ceilings

- The space-only field is unresolved geography. It may combine population history, dispersal, unmeasured environment and sampling geometry, and is not neutral genetic divergence.
- An excess shows that geographical proximity alone does not reproduce the environmental ordering of the white–pigmented switch. It does not demonstrate selection, local adaptation, drift, or a unique causal environmental mechanism.
- The absence of excess for conditional intensity has two readings that the present data do not separate: environmental tracking that is spatially smooth, and low power of a distance-based contrast against the retained Temperature PC1 × seasonality interaction surface, on which environmentally distant locations can share a prediction. Both are stated.
- The diagnostic in §1.2 is descriptive of this study region and does not generalise to regions where climate is more strongly organised by distance.

## 8. Implementation

`--geo-bins` already exists in `scripts/fit_broad_space_null_phenotype_excess.R`. Two additions:

1. a `--drop-nearest-bin` flag (or equivalent) applied when aggregating strata into the global statistic;
2. **write per-draw, per-stratum null contrasts to disk.** The script currently stores `null_median` per stratum in `matched_distance_stratum_contrasts.csv` but aggregates the 500 realisations before writing. Persisting the full stratum × draw matrix would let any stratum subset be evaluated later without refitting, which is the expensive step. This is the single change with the most future value.

Lock a stage `run_broad_space_null_geobin_sensitivity` with the six runs, their seeds and output checksums.
