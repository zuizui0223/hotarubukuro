# JBI background architecture: make the later analyses feel inevitable

## Editorial objective

The Introduction should not preview four independent mini-papers. It should make the reader feel that each later analysis becomes necessary only after the preceding problem has been solved.

The background is therefore built around **three nested bottlenecks in trait biogeography** rather than around methods:

1. **Measurement bottleneck** — range-wide traits are often available only as coarse morph classes or sparse quantitative measurements.
2. **Attribution bottleneck** — once a detailed national trait map exists, abiotic geography, spatial history and environmentally predicted biotic distributions covary across the same map.
3. **Exception bottleneck** — once a broad natural template is established, locally surprising observations still cannot be called biological anomalies from residual magnitude alone.

These bottlenecks are dependent. Better measurement exposes broad spatial structure; broad spatial structure makes same-scale pollinator correlations harder to interpret; the broad natural model is also what makes a calibrated local-departure question possible.

## Background sequence

### Opening hook — maps are easy to overinterpret

Start with a general biogeographical problem, not YAMAP and not *Campanula*:

> Geographical trait variation is easy to map and easy to overinterpret because physiology, history and selection can generate overlapping spatial patterns.

This gives JBI readers the general conceptual problem immediately.

### Gap 1 — the trait itself is often too coarse

Use flower colour as the exemplar. Contrast:

- broad categorical morph geography;
- small-sample quantitative spectroscopy;
- the missing middle: broad, quantitative, individual-level visible colour.

Introduce the biological reason to split pigmentation **state** from pigmented-only **intensity**. This is not merely a statistical convenience; it generates a prediction later: a pollinator association could concern maintenance of a visible signal state without implying progressive darkening among pigmented flowers.

### Solution 1 — repurpose an alternative image stream

Introduce YAMAP only after the measurement gap is clear. The reader should think: "we need many recent, georeferenced flower photographs" before being told where they came from.

Main-text YAMAP claims:

- hiking/recreation rather than biodiversity reporting;
- GPS/activity-linked mountain photographs;
- several-fold more matched-period focal-species records than iNaturalist in the locked benchmark;
- exhaustive author screening and deterministic trait construction;
- mountain sampling is targeted for natural trait geography but remains route/access/conspicuousness biased.

Full database comparison and QC belong in Appendix S1/S2.

### Gap 2 — solving measurement creates spatial-confounding trouble

This is the crucial hinge.

Once a national quantitative flower-colour map is possible, the obvious next move would be to regress it against climate, space and Bombus SDMs. But Bombus SDMs are themselves generated from environmental geography. Therefore a national Bombus coefficient can be statistically estimable while remaining biologically ambiguous.

The background should explicitly say that **scale is part of the hypothesis**. The broad environmental/spatial model is needed first to establish the geographical template. The pollinator question then changes scale and comparison unit to abrupt nearby colour boundaries.

### Why this species makes the local test possible

Only now introduce the focal natural history in detail:

- large tubular flowers;
- strong prior evidence for bumblebee pollination;
- realized Bombus assemblage turnover affects pollen-transfer ecology in *C. punctata*;
- *B. ardens* and *B. diversus* are the directly documented broad focal pollinators;
- high-elevation taxa are a confounding guardrail because their niches overlap pigmented highlands.

State the directional pigmentation-benefit relaxation hypothesis and its prediction for **state more directly than intensity**.

### Gap 3 — a strong natural template creates the anomaly question

The broad model is not just a baseline for Bombus. It also creates the reference required to ask what counts as a local departure.

Do not introduce horticulture as a fourth driver. Instead:

1. define a biologically readable local event independently of human variables;
2. ask whether the natural model itself generates the same event;
3. only then use ornamental cultivation to motivate a provenance follow-up.

This keeps the human layer dependent on the broad layer rather than appearing as another predictor family.

### Final paragraph — three linked questions, one dependent argument

The Introduction should end by making the dependency explicit:

- quantitative trait construction makes the national template possible;
- the national template reveals why a same-scale Bombus overlap is ambiguous and motivates a local boundary test;
- the same template supplies the predictive reference needed to calibrate local departures before examining human context.

The paper therefore asks three linked questions, but they are not three parallel analyses.

## Foreshadowing that must be paid off later

| Background clue | Later payoff |
|---|---|
| state and intensity are biologically distinct | Bombus signal, if any, concerns state rather than darker pigmented flowers |
| YAMAP is mountain-focused | strength for natural geography; possible compression of human gradient in final layer |
| national maps share geography | motivates local Bombus boundary design instead of an omnibus national regression |
| high-elevation Bombus overlap pigmented highlands | equal-elevation guardrail reveals shared biogeography |
| ornamental cultivation is only a possibility | human context is examined only after candidates are fixed and natural-map calibrated |
| residual magnitude is not an ecological event | repeated predictive maps distinguish field targets from demonstrated extra-process anomalies |

## Compression rule

Every Introduction paragraph must do at least one of four jobs:

1. establish a general biogeographical problem;
2. expose a specific limitation in existing evidence;
3. make the next design choice logically necessary;
4. plant a clue that is paid off in Results/Discussion.

If a paragraph mainly documents robustness or platform detail, move it to Supporting Information.
