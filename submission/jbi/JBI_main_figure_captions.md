# Journal of Biogeography — main figure captions

## Figure 1. From hiking photographs to a two-part national flower-colour phenotype

(A) Recreational YAMAP activity photographs were converted to quantitative traits through linked date/coordinate provenance, exhaustive author validation of taxon, focal flower and petal region, duplicate/coordinate audit, deterministic sRGB-to-CIELAB conversion and optical quality control. (B) Spatial distribution of the 1,922 final phenotype observations; point fill shows the extracted visible petal colour rather than a platform colour label. (C) Response-blind four-component Gaussian mixture of CIELAB a*, collapsed across the largest gap in component means into white-like and pigmented regimes. The dashed line is the posterior 0.5 pigmentation boundary at a*=4.968780. (D) Conditional visible intensity among the 956 observations classified as pigmented; white-like a* variation is excluded rather than interpreted as low anthocyanin concentration. The measurements are based on uncalibrated display-referred sRGB and do not quantify pigment chemistry, ultraviolet contrast or pollinator-perceived colour.

## Figure 2. Broad environmental and continuous spatial structure of flower colour

(A) Posterior environmental coefficients from the final observation-level INLA-SPDE models. Pigmentation state retains the additive environmental model; pigmented-only intensity includes Temperature PC1 × temperature seasonality because this interaction improved both posterior fit and transfer to withheld geography. Its negative coefficient means that the warm-climate decline in visible intensity becomes stronger as long-term annual temperature variability increases. Topography PC1 retains a separate negative association with conditional intensity. Intervals are 95% credible intervals conditional on the structural East/West adjustment and stationary spatial field. (B) Cross-fitted pigmentation-state probability from the final eight-axis natural reference used by the current downstream local-departure analysis. (C) Cross-fitted conditional-intensity prediction among cells containing pigmented observations. (D) Residual Matérn spatial ranges and geographically blocked predictive performance. VPD/site-water-balance additions and coastline-barrier SPDEs did not improve transfer sufficiently to replace the retained models. Spatial ranges are residual-correlation scales, not dispersal distances or identified population-history boundaries.

## Figure 3. A scale shift from national geography to sharp local focal-pollinator boundaries

(A) Sixty-seven non-overlapping pure white-to-pigmented cell pairs selected within 5 km without reading *Bombus* values, environmental values or the eventual contrast direction. (B) The already fixed transitions were closer than eligible local edges in the finalized eight-axis Broad abiotic state space: median RMS distance 0.244 versus 0.318 for all local edges (ratio 0.769). This is a balance diagnostic, not a matching criterion; the historical four-PC diagnostic gives the same ordering. (C) Pigmented-minus-white difference in occurrence-referenced support from the documented broad focal guild, max(*Bombus ardens*, *B. diversus*). The mean was +0.03590, but the median was -0.00277 and 49.3% of pairs were positive, showing that the nominal one-sided sign-flip result (P=0.02716; BH q=0.08148 across the 5/10/25-km pure tests) was magnitude-driven rather than a majority-of-pairs shift. (D) The directional mean attenuated at 10 and 25 km and did not reproduce on the raw cloglog scale; all-five and montane/alpine occurrence-referenced maxima were also non-significant at 5 km. Predicted habitat support is not abundance, visitation, pollen transfer or selection pressure, so the result is weak local directional consistency rather than evidence of pollinator-mediated selection.

## Figure 4. Calibrated local ecological departures and the remaining provenance question

(A) The current primary event is a pigmented 1-km cell embedded among at least three cells within 10 km that have standardized RMS environmental distance <=1 across the same eight measured abiotic axes used in the finalized Broad pigmentation-state analysis and contain only observed white flowers; human variables and residual thresholds do not enter event definition. (B) Locations of the **16** current-Broad field/provenance targets. (C) Observed candidate count and fraction relative to 10,000 checksum-locked cross-fitted maps from the final-eight-axis natural state model. Candidate count (16; null mean 13.59; P=0.27897) and candidate fraction (0.04071; null mean 0.03107; upper-tail P=0.12609) are not excessive. (D) Post-selection human-context contrasts under the same current-Broad detector. Population exposure within 5 km is the leading feature (focal-minus-white-neighbour contrast +0.06744; directional P=0.00800), but global maxT FWER P=0.05479 remains just above 0.05. Population at 10 km and DID proximity point in the same direction without corrected support, while 25–50-km population contrasts are weak. Observation-effort and independent-site-support alternatives are null after maxT correction. Thus the result is a near-threshold short-scale settlement-exposure signal, not evidence of an additional anthropogenic process or horticultural provenance; the sites remain priorities for field history, vouchers and population-genetic follow-up.
