# Journal of Biogeography — main figure captions

## Figure 1. From hiking photographs to a two-part national flower-colour phenotype

(A) Every YAMAP candidate returned by the predefined focal-name keyword frame was author-screened for taxon identity, focal flower and usable petal region; identical-coordinate records and SHA-256 image hashes were audited before deterministic sRGB-to-CIELAB conversion and optical quality control. (B) Spatial distribution of the 1,922 final phenotype observations; point fill shows the extracted visible petal colour rather than a platform colour label. (C) Response-blind four-component Gaussian mixture of CIELAB a*, collapsed across the largest gap in component means into white-like and pigmented regimes. The dashed line is the posterior 0.5 pigmentation boundary at a*=4.968780. (D) Conditional visible intensity among the 956 observations classified as pigmented; white-like a* variation is excluded rather than interpreted as low anthocyanin concentration. The measurements are based on uncalibrated display-referred sRGB and do not quantify pigment chemistry, ultraviolet contrast or pollinator-perceived colour.

## Figure 2. Broad environmental and continuous spatial structure of flower colour

(A) Posterior environmental coefficients from the national observation-level INLA-SPDE models. A one-SD shift toward warmer Temperature PC1 corresponded to pigmentation odds ratio 0.58 (95% CrI 0.36–0.95) and a 0.319-SD decline in pigmented-only intensity; intensity also declined by 0.138 SD along the slope–roughness–TRI axis, which is a terrain-complexity axis rather than elevation. Precipitation, seasonality, soil and shortwave-radiation intervals included zero. Coefficients are conditional on the structural East/West factor and continuous spatial field. (B) Cross-fitted natural-model pigmentation probability across 1-km cells. (C) Cross-fitted conditional-intensity prediction among cells containing pigmented observations. (D) Residual Matérn ranges were broader for pigmentation state (posterior mean 132.7 km) than intensity (60.9 km), although intervals overlapped, and geographically blocked prediction remained useful. The spatial field is unresolved geography, not a single historical mechanism.

## Figure 3. A scale shift from national geography to sharp local focal-pollinator boundaries

(A) Sixty-seven non-overlapping pure white-to-pigmented cell pairs selected within 5 km without reading Bombus values or the eventual contrast direction. (B) Selected transitions were more environmentally similar than the full set of eligible local edges in the predefined four-PC environmental space. (C) Pigmented-minus-white difference in occurrence-referenced support from the documented broad focal guild, max(*Bombus ardens*, *B. diversus*). The mean was +0.03590, but the median was -0.00277 and 49.3% of pairs were positive, showing that the nominal one-sided sign-flip result (P=0.02716; BH q=0.08148 across the 5/10/25-km pure tests) was magnitude-driven rather than a majority-of-pairs shift. (D) The directional mean attenuated at 10 and 25 km and did not reproduce on the raw cloglog scale; all-five and montane/alpine occurrence-referenced maxima were also non-significant at 5 km. Predicted habitat support is not abundance, visitation, pollen transfer or selection pressure, so the result is weak local directional consistency rather than evidence of pollinator-mediated selection.

## Figure 4. Calibrated local ecological departures and the remaining provenance question

(A) The primary event was a pigmented 1-km cell embedded among at least three cells within 10 km that were environmentally similar in the predefined four-PC space and contained only observed white flowers. Human variables and residual thresholds did not enter event definition. (B) Locations of the 17 fixed field/provenance targets. (C) Observed candidate count and fraction relative to 10,000 cross-fitted natural predictive maps. Candidate count (P=0.19958) and fraction (P=0.08739) were not a robust excess; a 200,000-map joint posterior-predictive sensitivity was less extreme. (D) Candidate-minus-white-neighbour population and densely inhabited district (DID) contrasts, with 95% natural-map intervals and maxT familywise P values. The strongest short-scale contrasts were suggestive but familywise inconclusive (5-km population maxT P=0.08991; population–DID alignment maxT P=0.07592), while the broad multivariate human-context test was null. These sites are prioritized for field and genetic provenance work, not classified as anthropogenic or horticultural anomalies.
