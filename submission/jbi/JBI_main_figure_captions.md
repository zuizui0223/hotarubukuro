# Journal of Biogeography — main figure captions

## Figure 1. From hiking photographs to two parts of flower colour

Flower-colour phenotyping of *Campanula punctata* across Japan. (a) We screened YAMAP hiking photographs, confirmed the taxon, focal flower and petal region, audited duplicate images and coordinates, and converted sRGB colour to CIELAB with one fixed pipeline. (b) The 1,922 final observations across Japan; point fill shows the extracted visible petal colour. The map uses WGS84 longitude/latitude and includes a 100-km bar scale. (c) A four-component Gaussian mixture of CIELAB a* was fitted before geography or ecological predictors were used. The dashed line marks the white/pigmented boundary at a*=4.968780. (d) Visible colour intensity among the 956 pigmented observations. The study therefore separates whether pigmentation is present from how strong the colour is once pigmentation is present. The image measurements do not quantify pigment chemistry, UV contrast or pollinator-perceived colour.

## Figure 2. Pigmentation state and colour intensity follow different broad geography

Broad-scale environmental and spatial patterns of *Campanula punctata* across Japan. (a) Environmental coefficients from the final INLA-SPDE models. Pigmentation state uses an additive model. Colour intensity among pigmented flowers includes Temperature PC1 × temperature seasonality; the negative interaction means that colour declines more strongly toward warmer climates where annual temperature variability is greater. Intervals are 95% credible intervals. (b) Cross-fitted probability of pigmentation from the final eight-axis natural model. (c) Cross-fitted colour intensity among cells with pigmented observations. Map panels (b) and (c) use WGS84 longitude/latitude and include a 100-km bar scale. (d) Residual spatial ranges and prediction to held-out geographical blocks. The spatial ranges describe remaining correlation after measured environment; they are not dispersal distances or genetic boundaries.

## Figure 3. Local boundaries identify a short-range Bombus correspondence

Local white-pigmented boundaries of *Campanula punctata* and focal-bumblebee habitat support in mainland Japan. (a) Sixty-seven non-overlapping pure white-pigmented cell pairs within 5 km. The 5-km radius was the finest predeclared scale with enough replicated transitions and represents a local population-neighbourhood comparison, not an estimate of bumblebee foraging distance. Pairs were selected before Bombus values, environmental values or contrast direction were read. The map uses WGS84 longitude/latitude and includes a 100-km bar scale. (b) The fixed pairs were environmentally closer than ordinary local edges in the final eight-axis environment: median distance 0.244 versus 0.318. Environment did not select or weight the pairs. (c) Pigmented-minus-white difference in occurrence-referenced support for max(*Bombus ardens*, *B. diversus*). Mean support was +0.03590 higher on the pigmented side (one-sided P=0.02716), while the median was -0.00277 and 49.3% of pairs were positive (q=0.08148 across the 5/10/25-km primary family). (d) The mean contrast weakened at 10 and 25 km and was not reproduced by raw SDM support. Together, the panels localize the plausible biotic contribution to short-range pigmentation-state boundaries; the equal-elevation highland guardrail is reported in Appendix S5. The SDMs represent habitat opportunity, not visitation or selection.

## Figure 4. Pigmented isolation carries a human-context overlay beyond natural geography

Spatial geometry and human context of *Campanula punctata* across Japan. (a) All 1,305 flower-colour cells; grey points show white cells, while 674 pigmented cells are sized by distance to the nearest other pigmented cell and filled by population-exposure rank within 5 km. The map uses WGS84 longitude/latitude and includes a 100-km bar scale. Median nearest-pigmented distance was 3.61 km. (b) Population exposure versus density-corrected same-colour isolation, displayed in within-colour rank space. Pigmented rho was 0.285, whereas white rho was 0.079; local flower-cell spacing has therefore been removed before the comparison. (c) Observed 5-km correlations (filled points) against means and 95% intervals from 10,000 final-eight-axis natural maps (open points and bars). The pigmented relationship exceeded natural expectation for raw isolation (rho=0.252; natural mean 0.133; P=0.0002) and relative isolation (rho=0.285; natural mean 0.154; P=0.0009). The raw pigmented-minus-white difference was elevated, but its relative-isolation form was not, so a robust reciprocal colour displacement is not claimed. (d) Observed pigmented correlations and natural intervals across focal, 5-, 10-, 25- and 50-km population scales. The result indicates a pigmented-specific human-context overlay; it does not establish horticultural origin, introduction or gene flow.
