# Journal of Biogeography — main figure captions

## Figure 1. From hiking photographs to two parts of flower colour

Flower-colour phenotyping of *Campanula punctata* across Japan. (a) We screened YAMAP hiking photographs, confirmed the taxon, focal flower and petal region, audited duplicate images and coordinates, and converted sRGB colour to CIELAB with one fixed pipeline. (b) The 1,922 final observations across Japan; point fill shows the extracted visible petal colour. The map uses WGS84 longitude/latitude and includes a 100-km bar scale. (c) A four-component Gaussian mixture of CIELAB a* was fitted before geography or ecological predictors were used. The dashed line marks the white/pigmented boundary at a*=4.968780. (d) Visible colour intensity among the 956 pigmented observations. The study therefore separates whether pigmentation is present from how strong the colour is once pigmentation is present. The image measurements do not quantify pigment chemistry, UV contrast or pollinator-perceived colour.

## Figure 2. Pigmentation state and colour intensity follow different broad geography

Broad-scale environmental and spatial patterns of *Campanula punctata* across Japan. (a) Environmental coefficients from the final INLA-SPDE models. Pigmentation state uses an additive model. Colour intensity among pigmented flowers includes Temperature PC1 × temperature seasonality; the negative interaction means that colour declines more strongly toward warmer climates where annual temperature variability is greater. Intervals are 95% credible intervals. (b) Cross-fitted probability of pigmentation from the final eight-axis natural model. (c) Cross-fitted colour intensity among cells with pigmented observations. Map panels (b) and (c) use WGS84 longitude/latitude and include a 100-km bar scale. (d) Residual spatial ranges and prediction to held-out geographical blocks. The spatial ranges describe remaining correlation after measured environment; they are not dispersal distances or genetic boundaries.

## Figure 3. Local Bombus correspondence is weak and scale dependent

Local white-pigmented boundaries of *Campanula punctata* and focal-bumblebee habitat support in mainland Japan. (a) Sixty-seven non-overlapping pure white-pigmented cell pairs within 5 km. Pairs were selected before Bombus values, environmental values or contrast direction were read. The map uses WGS84 longitude/latitude and includes a 100-km bar scale. (b) The fixed pairs were environmentally closer than ordinary local edges in the final eight-axis environment: median distance 0.244 versus 0.318. Environment did not select or weight the pairs. (c) Pigmented-minus-white difference in occurrence-referenced support for max(*Bombus ardens*, *B. diversus*). The mean was +0.03590, but the median was -0.00277 and 49.3% of pairs were positive (one-sided P=0.02716; q=0.08148 across the 5/10/25-km primary family). (d) The mean contrast weakened at 10 and 25 km and was not reproduced by raw SDM support. The SDMs represent habitat opportunity, not visitation or selection.

## Figure 4. Local colour departures are calibrated before human context is tested

Local flower-colour departures of *Campanula punctata* across Japan. (a) A local event is a pigmented 1-km cell with at least three neighbours within 10 km that are environmentally similar across the final eight abiotic axes and contain only observed white flowers. Human variables do not enter the event rule. (b) The **16** observed field/provenance targets in Japan. The map uses WGS84 longitude/latitude and includes a 100-km bar scale. (c) Observed event count and fraction compared with 10,000 cross-fitted natural maps. Candidate count (16; null mean 13.59; P=0.27897) and candidate fraction (0.04071; null mean 0.03107; P=0.12609) are not unusually high. (d) Human context tested only after the 16 sites were fixed. Population exposure within 5 km is the strongest feature (+0.06744; directional P=0.00800), but global maxT FWER P=0.05479. The sites therefore motivate provenance and field follow-up; they are not evidence of human origin.
