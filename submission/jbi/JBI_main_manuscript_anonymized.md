# From broad geography to local boundaries: flower-colour biogeography from hiking photographs

**Running title:** Layered flower-colour biogeography

## Abstract

**Aim:** Geographical trait variation can reflect abiotic, historical, biotic and human processes that share spatial structure. We asked how these processes organize a quantitative flower-colour polymorphism when tested at ecologically defensible scales.

**Location:** Japan.

**Taxon:** *Campanula punctata* (Campanulaceae).

**Methods:** We converted author-screened GPS-linked YAMAP photographs from 2023–2025 into pigmentation state and pigmented-only visible intensity. National INLA-SPDE models tested environment and residual geography. We then tested Bombus-blind white–pigmented transitions within 5 km against occurrence-referenced habitat support for *Bombus ardens* and *B. diversus*. Finally, local pigmented departures were defined in the finalized eight-axis abiotic state space, replayed on 10,000 cross-fitted natural maps, and only then characterized for human context.

**Results:** Among 1,922 photographs, pigmentation probability declined toward warmer climates. Pigmented-only intensity depended on Temperature PC1 × temperature seasonality and was weaker toward wetter and more rugged environments. Across 67 sharp transitions, mean focal-bumblebee support was higher on the pigmented side, but the effect was magnitude-driven, had a near-zero median and weakened across exposure and scale sensitivities. The fixed pairs remained closer than local edges in finalized abiotic space. Sixteen local departures were not excessive under natural maps. Population exposure within 5 km was the leading human feature (directional P=0.0080), but global maxT FWER P=0.0548.

**Main conclusions:** Floral colour polymorphism was ecologically layered rather than a single white-to-dark axis: broad climate and residual geography were associated with whether pigmentation was expressed, whereas intensity after pigmentation followed a different climate-context response. Any focal-bumblebee contribution appears weak, local and more consistent with maintenance of a pigmented state than progressive darkening. Human context provides a near-threshold short-scale settlement signal, not evidence of anthropogenic origin. Macroecological trait geography can therefore narrow where adaptive, demographic and contemporary human mechanisms should be tested directly.

**Keywords:** digital phenotyping, flower colour, hiking photographs, iEcology, intraspecific variation, pollination, spatial confounding, trait biogeography

## Introduction

Geographical variation within species is not merely descriptive noise around a species mean. Intraspecific trait variation can alter individual performance, population persistence, species interactions and the ecological breadth of populations across heterogeneous environments (Westerband et al., 2021). Understanding how such variation is organized across a species range is therefore central to explaining local adaptation and the geographical mosaic of ecological interactions. Yet the same phenotype can be shaped by several processes at once: abiotic conditions, dispersal and population history, biotic interactions and, increasingly, human movement or habitat modification. These alternatives matter because they imply different mechanisms and different expectations for how trait distributions should respond when environments or interacting species change.

Flower colour is an unusually integrative trait for addressing this problem. Floral pigmentation is simultaneously a physiological phenotype and a reproductive signal: pigments and floral optics can covary with abiotic conditions, while visible colour can influence pollinator attraction, mating patterns and reproductive success (Rausher, 2008; Koski & Ashman, 2015; Trunschke et al., 2021). Geographic colour polymorphisms therefore provide a tractable window into how multiple selective agents and spatial history combine within one conspicuous phenotype. But that integrative potential creates an inferential danger. A climate gradient, a pollinator turnover and a historical population boundary can generate similar spatial patterns, so forcing all candidate processes into one same-scale regression can mistake shared geography for mechanism. The ecological need is therefore not simply for more predictors, but for an integrated design in which each process is examined at the spatial scale and comparison unit at which its biological meaning is most defensible; biotic interactions are especially scale dependent (Araújo & Rozenfeld, 2014).

That integration first requires a phenotype measured densely enough to reveal the geography that subsequent mechanisms must explain. Range-wide flower-colour studies often rely on discrete morph frequencies, whereas quantitative reflectance or pigment measurements are usually available from fewer populations (Arista et al., 2013; Koski & Galloway, 2020). This matters because a single white-to-pink axis can merge biologically distinct transitions. A threshold-like loss or activation of anthocyanin-pathway expression can separate white and visibly pigmented flowers, whereas variation among already pigmented flowers can additionally reflect pigment amount, pigment chemistry and floral optics (Dick et al., 2011; van der Kooi et al., 2016; Tasaki et al., 2022). Distinguishing whether pigmentation is expressed from how intense that pigmentation becomes is therefore necessary not only for measurement precision, but also for asking whether different ecological processes act on different components of the phenotype.

Anthocyanin-associated floral colour is environmentally responsive. Experimental work shows that moderate low temperature can increase floral anthocyanin accumulation and pathway expression, while geographical studies report stronger pigmentation or lower reflectance in cooler climates; drought, atmospheric demand and light can provide additional routes (Shvarts et al., 1997; Koski & Galloway, 2020; Sullivan & Koski, 2021). In *C. punctata* itself, UV-B exclusion reduces leaf flavonoid accumulation, although that result concerns leaves rather than petal anthocyanin (Hashiba et al., 2006). We therefore treated warm-season thermal regime as the primary directional abiotic hypothesis, while moisture, radiation, climatic seasonality, terrain relief and soil represented additional stress or resource contexts. Because the effect of a long-term mean climate can depend on climatic variability, we also evaluated biologically motivated interactions rather than assuming every environmental coefficient was additive.

Digital imagery offers a way to bridge the gap between broad categorical maps and small-sample quantitative measurements. Biodiversity platforms increasingly provide photographs from which visible phenotypes can be recovered (Laitly et al., 2021; Luong et al., 2023; McKenzie et al., 2026), while iEcology extends this idea to digital material originally created for other purposes (Jarić et al., 2020). We used YAMAP, a hiking-navigation and activity-diary platform, because public hiking activities can retain route-linked photograph locations across mountain systems. In a matched 2023–2025 Supplementary benchmark, the author-screened YAMAP retrieval yielded 1,964 georeferenced focal-species records compared with 516 iNaturalist observations with photographs; the matched GBIF image set was almost entirely syndicated from iNaturalist (Appendix S1). This does not make YAMAP unbiased. Its observation process is different: users document hikes rather than submit focal-species records, potentially reducing selection conditioned specifically on focal-taxon reporting while retaining route choice, access, flower conspicuousness and subject-selection biases. For a mountain herb, this sampling frame is concentrated in natural and semi-natural mountain environments relevant to wild-population geography, although it cannot establish wild provenance for every photograph.

Once this measurement problem is solved, integration becomes more—not less—important. A detailed national flower-colour map exposes the fact that climate, topography, residual population history and predicted pollinator distributions can all share the same broad geography. Historical work on *C. punctata* shows mainland–Izu genetic differentiation and progressive island colonization, demonstrating that present-day geography can carry biological memory not reducible to current climate (Inoue & Kawahara, 1990). At the same time, species-distribution models (SDMs) are themselves generated from environmental predictors, so a national flower-colour–pollinator association can be statistically precise while remaining biologically ambiguous (Soberón, 2007; Paciorek, 2010; Hanks et al., 2015). An integrated analysis must therefore connect scales rather than collapse them: broad environment and continuous space should first establish the geographical template, after which a local biotic hypothesis can be asked at abrupt nearby boundaries.

*Campanula punctata* provides strong natural-history grounds for such a local test. Its large tubular flowers are effectively pollinated by bumblebees, and realized *Bombus* assemblages vary geographically. In this species, flower–pollinator size matching can affect pollen removal and floral trait selection (Nagano et al., 2014), while in the Izu Islands the loss of bumblebee-dominated pollination is associated with major changes in reproductive ecology and breeding system (Inoue & Amano, 1986; Inoue, 1988). These studies do not show that bumblebees determine white versus pigmented flowers, but they make a directional colour hypothesis biologically meaningful. If visible pigmentation contributes to attraction or detection by important bumblebee visitors, the reproductive benefit of maintaining a pigmented state could be greater where those pollinators are locally available and relax where their availability declines. This prediction concerns the maintenance of a visible pigment state more directly than progressive darkening among flowers that are already pigmented. We therefore focus the directional test on the broadly distributed, directly documented focal pollinators *B. ardens* and *B. diversus*. Montane and alpine *Bombus* species are retained as guardrails against mistaking shared mountain biogeography for an additional pollinator mechanism.

The broad natural template is also ecologically important because it defines what should count as exceptional. Without such a reference, a locally unusual colour state can be interpreted too readily as evidence of a new process. A large fitted residual is not automatically a biological anomaly, because residual magnitude also reflects model uncertainty, leverage and sampling geometry. This distinction is especially relevant in *C. punctata*, which is cultivated ornamentally and may therefore experience planting, escape or introgression in addition to natural environmental and biotic processes. Human influence is consequently not treated as a competing national predictor. We first ask whether a locally discordant colour configuration is unusual relative to repeated natural predictive maps, and only then examine whether independently defined candidates occupy distinctive human context.

These questions form a hierarchy of hypotheses about the ecological differentiation and maintenance of flower-colour polymorphism rather than parallel searches for significant predictors. Broad environmental geography can reveal where pigmentation state and intensity are consistently associated with different abiotic settings, but cannot by itself distinguish plasticity, local adaptation or historical sorting. Local pollinator opportunity can then ask whether reproductive benefits plausibly modify the maintenance of pigmentation at a finer scale, while contemporary human context can be examined only after natural geographical expectations have been established. We therefore ask: (1) how thermal, moisture, radiative, terrain, soil and unresolved spatial geography organize pigmentation state and conditional visible intensity across Japan; (2) within that template, whether the sharpest nearby white–pigmented transitions align directionally with predicted habitat opportunity for focal bumblebee pollinators; and (3) whether locally discordant pigmented populations are more frequent than expected under the finalized natural state model and, once defined independently, occupy distinctive human context. Together, these analyses use macroecological pattern to refine adaptive and historical hypotheses, not to claim direct evidence of selection.

## Materials and Methods

### Digital sampling and two-part flower-colour phenotype

The predefined sampling frame covered the flowering seasons of 2023–2025. All YAMAP records returned by the fixed Japanese focal-name search frame, including “ホタルブクロ”, were taken through author screening rather than subsampled. During screening, taxonomic misidentifications and non-focal campanuloid subjects were excluded, and the focal flower and usable petal region were confirmed. Records sharing identical coordinates were audited against photographs, dates and activity-level provenance; coordinate identity alone was not an exclusion criterion. Exact-image duplication was audited separately from SHA-256 hashes.

The screened source table contained 1,965 eligible records, including 1,964 YAMAP activity-photo rows representing 1,963 unique YAMAP image hashes. After removal of one later exact-image duplicate and 42 records lacking complete topographic (n=40) or soil (n=2) raster support, 1,922 observations entered the environment-complete integrated analyses. The study treated these as route-linked photographs, not as an areally random population survey.

The deterministic image pipeline retained source-row provenance, dates, coordinates and image hashes, summarized display-referred sRGB pixels within the confirmed petal region and converted median RGB to CIELAB under D65. CIELAB a* was used as a reproducible human-visible red–green phenotype, not as calibrated reflectance, ultraviolet contrast or anthocyanin concentration. Full data-source and image-QC details are in Appendices S1–S2.

The white–pigmented boundary was estimated from a* alone without geography, environment, *Bombus* predictions or human context. Univariate Gaussian mixtures were compared by BIC and ordered by fitted mean; the selected four-component variable-variance model was collapsed at the largest adjacent mean gap. The operational boundary was a*=4.9688. The final analysis contained 966 white-like and 956 pigmented observations; 124 observations with intermediate regime-membership probability were retained with an ambiguity flag. We analysed (i) binary pigmentation state for all 1,922 observations and (ii) standardized visible intensity only among pigmented observations.

### Broad environmental and spatial template

Climate predictors came from CHELSA v2.1, soils from SoilGrids 2.0 and terrain derivatives from a WorldClim 2.1 elevation surface (Karger et al., 2017; Brun et al., 2022; Poggio et al., 2021; Fick & Hijmans, 2017). Response-blind principal components summarized warm-season temperature/thermal accumulation, climatic moisture, terrain relief and soil resource/texture structure; temperature seasonality, precipitation seasonality and shortwave radiation entered directly. Elevation itself was not entered as an additional fixed effect because it jointly proxies several climatic and terrain processes already represented by the environmental terms.

We fitted separate observation-level INLA-SPDE models for pigmentation state and conditional intensity. Pigmentation state used a Bernoulli likelihood with logit link and conditional intensity a Gaussian likelihood. A Matérn field represented continuous unresolved geography after measured environment (Lindgren et al., 2011), with penalised-complexity priors on range and spatial variance (Simpson et al., 2017). The field is an agnostic residual biogeographic component that may contain unmeasured environment, dispersal/population history and sampling geography; its range is not interpreted as a dispersal distance.

We audited the environmental model in two stages (Appendix S3). First, ten ecologically motivated interactions were evaluated under the same likelihood, fixed main effects, mesh and five response-blind geographical folds; a second guardrail fitted all 28 possible pairwise products among the eight environmental axes one at a time. Interaction promotion required posterior support plus improved prediction to withheld geography, spatial-block bootstrap support and fold consistency. VIF was treated as a diagnostic rather than a mechanical deletion threshold: values <5 were preferred, values of 5–10 required explicit coefficient, spatial and blocked-transfer stability, and >10 prevented promotion without exceptional justification. A narrow joint fit resolved overlapping interaction signals. Second, CHELSA VPD and site water balance tested whether atmospheric demand or integrated water balance added information beyond the primary moisture axis. Alternative spatial specifications compared the stationary field with a coastline-barrier field and tested whether the structural East/West term was needed. A model was not promoted by WAIC alone.

For the later local-departure analysis, the predictive reference was aligned directly with the finalized pigmentation-state environment: the same eight measured abiotic axes were used in the cross-fitted state model, with five geographical folds based on approximately 100-km blocks (Roberts et al., 2017; Valavi et al., 2019). The resulting predictive maps were reused in the local-event replay. East/West remained a structural geographical adjustment in the observation-level Broad model rather than an abiotic matching dimension; the SPDE field belonged to the natural predictive reference rather than to measured-environment distance.

### Bumblebee SDMs and local sharp-transition test

Fresh SDMs were built for five focal Japanese *Bombus* species over a common Honshu–Shikoku–Kyushu domain using shared predictors, `maxnet`, spatial block partitions and finite-AICc model selection. These surfaces represent predicted habitat support rather than abundance, visitation or pollen transfer (Renner & Warton, 2013; Guillera-Arroita, 2015). A response-blind common predictor screen used VIF=10 as a predictive-design diagnostic; individual SDM coefficients were not interpreted as ecological effects. Because raw values are not directly comparable among species, each flower-cell prediction was mapped to its empirical rank relative to predictions at that species' retained occurrence cells. The primary exposure was the maximum occurrence-referenced support of *B. ardens* and *B. diversus*, the two broadly distributed focal pollinators with direct support in the *C. punctata* system. Full SDM calibration and all-five alternatives are in Appendices S4–S5.

The main pollinator test intentionally changed scale. At the 1-km-cell level, we connected up to the five nearest eligible neighbours within 5 km and identified pure white–pigmented transitions, defined as an absolute difference of one in observed cell pigment share. Pair selection was blind to *Bombus* values, environmental values and transition direction; non-overlapping pairs were selected before being oriented from white to pigmented. For each pair, we calculated pigmented-minus-white focal-pollinator support. The primary statistic was the mean contrast across pairs, tested with 100,000 sign flips.

Environment did not select, orient or weight transition pairs. As a descriptive balance diagnostic, the already fixed pairs were compared with all local graph edges in the same eight standardized abiotic axes used by the finalized Broad pigmentation-state model. Predefined *Bombus* sensitivities repeated the design at 10 and 25 km, relaxed the transition threshold and substituted raw SDM support. A supplementary guardrail tested whether support from the three montane/alpine *Bombus* species remained associated with pigmentation when transition endpoints differed by no more than 50 or 100 m elevation. Five-species community turnover and full spatial matching are retained in Appendix S5 because turnover is unsigned and does not predict which side of a boundary should be pigmented.

### Event-based local departures and human context

Rather than thresholding fitted residuals, we defined an ecological event in the finalized Broad state space: a pigmented cell with at least three neighbours within 10 km whose root-mean-square distance across the eight standardized abiotic axes was <=1 and whose eligible neighbours contained no observed pigmented flowers. Human variables did not enter this definition.

The identical event detector was replayed on 10,000 checksum-locked cross-fitted predictive maps from the final-eight-axis pigmentation-state model while cell geometry and observed trial counts were held fixed.

Only after candidate identities were fixed did we characterize human context using population at focal, 5-, 10-, 25- and 50-km scales, densely inhabited district (DID) proximity, road proximity, built land, forest–human interface context, forest cover and mountainness. The final family removed near-duplicate composites and placed all eleven features in one global maxT family. The same candidate detector and local human contrasts were recalculated for every natural predictive map, propagating uncertainty in which cells would be selected under the fitted natural geography. Observation-effort and independent-site-support ranks were analysed separately as sampling-process alternatives.

All manuscript-facing analyses, seeds and output provenance are versioned; an anonymized peer-review data/code package will accompany submission.

## Results

### Broad geography separates pigmentation state from conditional intensity

The 1,922 photographs were nearly evenly divided between white-like (966) and pigmented (956) flowers. In the final pigmentation-state model, the probability of pigmentation declined toward the warmer end of Temperature PC1 (posterior mean log-odds -0.542; 95% CrI -1.033 to -0.049; odds ratio about 0.58 per SD). No environmental interaction passed the complete posterior-plus-geographical-transfer rule for state. A dryness × shortwave-radiation interaction was suggestive, but its spatial-block predictive-gain interval included zero and it remained Supporting Information only.

Among pigmented flowers, the final model retained a Temperature PC1 × temperature-seasonality interaction (mean -0.204; 95% CrI -0.302 to -0.107). Thus the association between warmer conditions and weaker visible intensity became more negative as long-term annual temperature variability increased. At the standardized reference seasonality the Temperature PC1 main effect was weak and uncertain (-0.084; -0.274 to 0.106), so the result is not a single constant national temperature slope. Conditional intensity also declined toward wetter/moister Precipitation PC1 (-0.174; -0.323 to -0.024) and toward steeper terrain with greater local elevation relief (-0.134; -0.224 to -0.043).

The exhaustive interaction audit initially identified precipitation PC1 × temperature seasonality as well, but that coefficient collapsed toward zero when fitted jointly with the predeclared thermal interaction, whereas Temperature PC1 × temperature seasonality remained supported. Adding long-term VPD or site water balance did not improve geographical transfer sufficiently to expand either primary model; VPD produced severe fixed-effect collinearity (maximum VIF about 26). Similarly, coastline-barrier SPDEs did not improve held-out geography, despite documented mainland–Izu population differentiation. We therefore retained stationary Matérn fields with the structural East/West adjustment.

Substantial residual geography remained. Posterior mean SPDE range was 132.8 km (95% CrI 88.8–195.7) for pigmentation state and 65.7 km (31.0–132.6) for the final conditional-intensity model. These are residual correlation scales, not seed-, pollen- or colonization distances. The broader state range is descriptive only because likelihoods and response subsets differ.

### Sharp local colour boundaries show weak alignment with focal bumblebee habitat support

The strict 5-km graph yielded 67 non-overlapping pure white–pigmented transitions with median separation 2.0 km. In the finalized eight-axis Broad abiotic state space, their median environmental distance was 0.244 compared with 0.318 among all local graph edges. The same ordering held at 10 km (0.337 vs 0.429) and 25 km (0.435 vs 0.531). Because environment did not enter pair selection, this is a balance diagnostic rather than a matching criterion.

Occurrence-referenced focal-bumblebee support was, on average, 0.0359 higher on the pigmented side (one-sided sign-flip P=0.0272). The pattern was not a majority-of-pairs effect: median contrast was -0.0028 and only 49.3% of pairs were positive. Across the 5-, 10- and 25-km pure-transition family, q=0.0815; mean contrasts attenuated to +0.0084 at 10 km (P=0.325) and +0.0029 at 25 km (P=0.436), and raw-cloglog support did not reproduce the 5-km result (P=0.267). We therefore regard the result as weak, highly local consistency with the directional hypothesis rather than evidence of pollinator-mediated selection.

The apparent high-elevation association between montane/alpine *Bombus* support and pigmented flowers did not survive the elevation guardrail: among pure transitions with <=50 m endpoint elevation difference, mean pigmented-minus-white montane support was near zero or negative at 5, 10 and 25 km (all one-sided P>=0.755; Appendix S5). Five-species community turnover showed broader landscape correspondence with colour boundaries, but that analysis is unsigned and therefore does not strengthen the directional mechanism. No persuasive *Bombus* relationship was supported for conditional intensity among pigmented flowers.

### Local departures are field targets, not demonstrated anthropogenic anomalies

The current-Broad ecological-event definition identified 16 pigmented cells embedded among nearby white cells that were similar in the finalized eight-axis abiotic state space. Across 10,000 cross-fitted natural maps, the null mean candidate count was 13.59 (95% interval 7–21; P=0.279). Candidate fraction was likewise compatible with the reference (observed 0.0407; null mean 0.0311; upper-tail P=0.126). Thus the frequency of these local configurations did not require an additional process beyond the fitted natural geography.

Human context was examined only after candidate definition. The leading feature was population exposure within 5 km: candidate cells exceeded their local white comparators by 0.0674 rank units (directional P=0.0080), but global maxT FWER P=0.0548 remained just above 0.05. Population at 10 km (+0.0601; directional P=0.0137; FWER P=0.108) and DID proximity (+0.0590; P=0.0220; FWER P=0.159) pointed in the same direction, whereas population contrasts at 25 and 50 km were small. Built-up fraction was directionally positive but not independently supported after global correction; road proximity and forest–human edge were weaker.

Measured within-dataset effort did not distinguish the 16 candidates from their local white comparators after the same predictive-map correction: observation-effort maxT P=0.963 and independent-site-support maxT P=0.756. This weakens a simple explanation based on candidates merely having more photographs or more independent YAMAP activities, while leaving the broader possibility that populated or accessible landscapes are more likely to enter the YAMAP sampling frame at all.

## Discussion

### Flower-colour polymorphism is organized at more than one ecological layer

The central biological result is not simply that flower colour covaries with environment. The white–pigmented transition and visible intensity after pigmentation showed different geographical organizations, indicating that the polymorphism cannot be reduced to a single continuum from white to progressively darker flowers. Pigmentation state was associated most clearly with broad thermal geography and substantial residual regional structure, whereas conditional intensity depended on the interaction between mean thermal regime and temperature seasonality and also varied with moisture and terrain. This separation is consistent with a hierarchical phenotype in which the ecological processes governing whether pigmentation is expressed need not be identical to those modulating its visible intensity once expressed.

That distinction matters for the adaptive interpretation of flower-colour polymorphism. Floral pigmentation can couple physiological functions with reproductive signalling, and geographical colour polymorphisms can be maintained when the costs and benefits of alternative phenotypes vary across environments (Arista et al., 2013; Koski & Ashman, 2015; Trunschke et al., 2021). Our data do not demonstrate local adaptation: the observed geography could combine developmental plasticity, genetically differentiated populations, demographic sorting and selection on pigmentation or correlated traits. Nevertheless, the response-specific patterns narrow the adaptive problem. A broad cool-climate association with pigmentation state identifies thermal geography as a candidate selective or developmental context for the presence of visible pigmentation, whereas the conditional-intensity interaction points to a different question about how pigmentation expression is modulated once the pathway is active. Thus formation, maintenance and quantitative expression of floral colour need not be governed by the same ecological process.

### Broad geography defines a candidate adaptive landscape, but not adaptation itself

At the broad scale, warmer warm-season geography was associated with a lower probability of entering the pigmented regime. This pattern is consistent with temperature-sensitive regulation or accumulation of anthocyanin-associated colour, but the photographs cannot distinguish developmental plasticity from evolved population differences or direct selection on petal optical, physiological or correlated traits. Among pigmented flowers, the warm-climate decline in visible intensity was strongest where annual temperature seasonality was high, and wetter/moister climatic geography and greater terrain relief were associated with weaker intensity. These relationships identify environmental settings in which the ecological value or expression of pigmentation may differ; they do not establish that the corresponding macroclimatic variables are the proximate agents experienced during flower development.

The remaining spatial field is equally informative when interpreted at the correct resolution. Substantial residual geography persisted after measured environment, which is plausible in a species with documented mainland–Izu genetic differentiation, high mainland outcrossing and progressive island colonization (Inoue & Kawahara, 1990). Yet the SPDE range is not a dispersal distance or a genetic cline. It can absorb unmeasured environment, lineage history, gene flow, dispersal and sampling geography in combination. Rather than treating this unresolved component as a nuisance or assigning it post hoc to population history, the broad analysis identifies a second empirical target: population-genomic sampling can ask whether the residual flower-colour geography corresponds to ancestry, isolation by distance, admixture or other demographic structure.

This is also where the finite resolution of macroecological data is most useful rather than merely limiting. The 1-km environmental layers and contemporary photographs cannot recover flower-level temperature, moisture, illumination, genotype or lifetime fitness, but their national replication allows recurrent geographical structure to be distinguished from isolated local observations. The combination of a two-part phenotype, continuous spatial adjustment, geographically blocked transfer tests and explicit model guardrails therefore uses the available data to identify robust candidate gradients without converting correlation into adaptation. Common-garden or reciprocal-transplant experiments across the thermal and moisture gradients identified here, coupled to petal spectra, pigment chemistry, physiology and reproductive performance, can now test whether those geographical associations reflect plasticity, genetically based local adaptation or both.

### Local pollinator opportunity may modify maintenance of the pigmented state

The pollinator analysis asks a different ecological question from the broad environmental models. In *C. punctata*, geographical changes in realized *Bombus* fauna can alter pollinator–flower size matching and pollen removal, and island populations respond strongly to loss of bumblebee pollination in their reproductive ecology (Inoue & Amano, 1986; Inoue, 1988; Nagano et al., 2014). These observations make pollinator opportunity biologically relevant, but an environment-derived SDM cannot by itself measure visitation or selection. For that reason the analysis moved from national overlap to independently selected local white–pigmented boundaries.

The 5-km result is compatible with a limited maintenance hypothesis. If visible pigmentation contributes to attraction or detection by important bumblebee visitors, its reproductive benefit could be greater where those visitors are locally available, while the advantage of maintaining the pigmented state could relax where focal-pollinator opportunity declines. The weak mean contrast, near-zero median, 49% positive-pair fraction, failure on raw SDM support and attenuation at broader scales all prevent a stronger claim. Importantly, no persuasive *Bombus* relationship emerged for intensity among already-pigmented flowers. If the local association is biological, it therefore fits maintenance or loss of a visible pigment state more naturally than selection for progressive darkening.

The montane guardrail reinforces the same point from the opposite direction. Montane and alpine *Bombus* species overlapped pigmented highland flowers nationally, but the association disappeared when nearby white and pigmented endpoints were constrained to nearly equal elevation. A visually striking biogeographic overlap was therefore explained by shared mountain geography rather than promoted to a second pollinator mechanism. The next resolution step is direct: species-resolved visitation, floral contact, pollen deposition, seed set and colour-choice or manipulation experiments can test whether the predicted local opportunity corresponds to realized reproductive selection.

### Contemporary human context may locally overlay natural trait geography

The broad natural template also changes what a locally unusual colour state means. Sixteen pigmented cells were embedded among environmentally similar white neighbourhoods, but comparable configurations arose often enough in 10,000 natural predictive maps that their total frequency was not anomalous. Spatially structured natural variation can therefore generate some apparently exceptional local colour configurations without requiring a new process. This is precisely why the ecological event was defined independently of human variables rather than by selecting large residuals or conspicuously settled sites.

The ornamental use of *C. punctata* nevertheless makes contemporary human context biologically relevant. Horticultural availability can create propagule pressure and establishment opportunities (Dehnen-Schmutz et al., 2007; Davis et al., 2016), and coloured *C. punctata* material has been deliberately bred in cultivation (Choi et al., 2012). Population exposure within 5 km was the leading post-selection feature, with weaker concordant signals at 10 km and DID proximity, but its global familywise P=0.0548 remains just above the confirmatory threshold. The evidence therefore supports a provenance hypothesis rather than an anthropogenic-origin conclusion.

Human influence need not act only through planting. Settlement can also modify microclimate, shade, irrigation, substrate and disturbance, while roads and population density can alter observation opportunities. The null within-dataset effort diagnostics make a simple excess-photography explanation less likely but do not remove broader route-access bias. YAMAP's mountain-focused sampling is consequently both a strength and a limit: it enriches natural and semi-natural habitats for the broad geographical questions but compresses the full urban–rural gradient available to the human-context test. The 16 sites are therefore most useful as deliberately identified natural experiments where field provenance, management history, local environment and ancestry can be measured together.

### From macroecological polymorphism to testable adaptive mechanisms

Taken together, the results support a layered ecological interpretation of floral colour polymorphism rather than a single-driver explanation. Broad abiotic geography is associated with whether pigmentation is expressed and with a distinct set of controls on intensity; unresolved regional structure indicates that environmental differentiation is superimposed on demographic or other spatial processes; focal bumblebee opportunity, if relevant, appears most plausibly as a weak local modifier of pigmented-state maintenance; and contemporary human activity may add another local layer without being required to explain the overall frequency of departures. The same visible polymorphism can therefore integrate physiological, reproductive, historical and contemporary processes that act on different trait components and at different spatial scales.

The macroecological analysis does not close the adaptive question; it makes that question experimentally tractable. Broad thermal, moisture and terrain associations now define populations and environmental contrasts for common-garden and reciprocal-transplant tests of phenotype and fitness. The residual SPDE geography defines regions in which population genomics can test ancestry, isolation by distance and admixture, and genotype–environment analyses can ask whether environmental differentiation persists after neutral structure is accounted for. The focal-Bombus SDMs identify local boundaries where realized visitation, stigma contact, pollen deposition and reproductive success can be measured directly. Finally, the 16 local departures provide targeted populations for repeated field sampling, vouchers, planting histories and genomic comparison with neighbouring white populations and horticultural material.

This macro-to-micro progression is the main ecological payoff of the analysis. A national opportunistic image archive cannot observe selection, gene flow or individual fitness directly, but it can identify where each mechanism is most plausibly acting, at what spatial scale and on which component of the phenotype. For *C. punctata*, the geography of flower-colour polymorphism is therefore best viewed not as the product of one dominant selective agent, but as a spatial mosaic in which abiotic context, unresolved population history, local biotic interactions and possible recent human modification contribute at different resolutions. By resolving that mosaic, macroecological trait geography can move beyond describing variation and specify the populations, contrasts and mechanisms in which adaptive explanations should next be tested directly.
